# ECONARES: BUYER CONTACT PROTOCOL — NO PREMATURE DISCLOSURE

> "Never reveal your price until you know their needs. Information is leverage. Silence is strategy."

This checklist exists because commodity prices are highly variable — specifications, destination port, payment terms, and volume all shift the number. Revealing pricing before understanding the buyer's full requirements locks you into a weak position and invites comparison shopping before trust is built.

---

## PHASE 1: PRE-CONTACT RESEARCH (BEFORE THE CALL)

- [ ] Research the buyer's business — company size, industry, import history
- [ ] Identify key decision-makers and procurement contacts
- [ ] Check if they have existing supplier relationships (competition intel)
- [ ] Note any public price references or recent news about their operations
- [ ] Prepare your product range and rough capability notes (do NOT pre-share)
- [ ] Have your standard inquiry questions ready
- [ ] Set a personal rule: NO PRICING in initial outreach

---

## PHASE 2: OPENING THE CONVERSATION

### Your Opening Statement (模板)
> "Good day. We're actively supplying [commodity] to buyers across the region and we'd like to understand your requirements first so we can properly assess if we're a fit."

### Immediate Actions When Buyer Responds
- [ ] Confirm you are speaking with the right person (procurement/decision-maker)
- [ ] Ask if this is a good time or schedule a follow-up call
- [ ] Do NOT jump into pricing under any circumstance
- [ ] If asked "how much?" — deflect: "I'd like to understand your needs first so I can give you accurate figures"

### Deflection Phrases for "How Much?" Questions
- "Pricing depends on a few factors I'd like to clarify first — can I ask you a few questions?"
- "I'd need to know the specifications and delivery terms before I can quote accurately."
- "Let's make sure we're comparing the same product first. What specs are you working with?"
- "Our pricing is tailored per buyer — let me understand your situation."

---

## PHASE 3: REQUIREMENTS GATHERING (THE REAL WORK)

### PRODUCT SPECIFICATIONS
- [ ] Exact commodity type (e.g., coal: sub-bituminous? anthracite?; diesel: Euro 5? Ron 91/95?)
- [ ] Required grade/purity/calorific value
- [ ] Quantity needed per month or per shipment
- [ ] Tolerance levels or specific parameters they require
- [ ] Any certification or testing requirements (e.g., SGS, Bureau Veritas)

### DESTINATION AND LOGISTICS
- [ ] Destination port or delivery location
- [ ] Preferred shipping schedule / frequency
- [ ] Incoterms preference (CIF, FOB, CFR, etc.)
- [ ] Are they equipped to receive bulk or do they need bagging/packaging?
- [ ] Any restricted access ports or terminals they use?

### PAYMENT TERMS
- [ ] What payment terms can they offer? (Letter of Credit? T/T? Net 30?)
- [ ] What is their standard procurement payment cycle?
- [ ] Do they require any credit arrangements or open accounts?
- [ ] Are there any financial constraints or approval processes?

### VOLUME AND DURATION
- [ ] What is the estimated monthly/quarterly volume?
- [ ] Is this a one-time purchase or recurring requirement?
- [ ] What is the buying cycle or budget period?
- [ ] Any seasonality to their demand?

### CURRENT SUPPLIER INTELLIGENCE
- [ ] Who are they currently buying from?
- [ ] What are they paying currently? (if shareable)
- [ ] What are the pain points with current supplier?
- [ ] When does their current contract expire?

### COMPETITION AND MARKET CONTEXT
- [ ] Have they received other offers? From whom?
- [ ] Are they actively benchmarking or just exploring?
- [ ] What is their decision timeline?
- [ ] Who else is involved in the decision?

---

## PHASE 4: INFORMATION YOU DO NOT REVEAL (HARD RULES)

### NEVER Disclose in Early Stages:
- [ ] Specific pricing or discount levels
- [ ] Your cost structure or margins
- [ ] Names of other buyers or volume commitments
- [ ] Exact availability dates until confirmed
- [ ] Alternative products or substitute options (unless asked)
- [ ] Internal supplier negotiations or offers you've received

### Rationale:
| Information | Why Withheld |
|---|---|
| Pricing | Price is a function of variables they haven't disclosed yet. Early reveal invites low-ball counteroffers and comparison. |
| Margins | Revealing cost structure eliminates negotiating power permanently. |
| Other buyers | Confidentiality is trust. Breaching it signals unreliability. |
| Substitutes | Offering alternatives before understanding their constraints is premature. |

---

## PHASE 5: CLOSING THE QUALIFICATION CALL

### Before Ending the Call:
- [ ] Summarize the requirements you've gathered back to the buyer
- [ ] Confirm you have everything needed to provide a serious offer
- [ ] Agree on next steps and timeline
- [ ] Ask permission to follow up with a formal proposal
- [ ] Set a specific callback or proposal delivery date
- [ ] Get their preferred contact method and best time to reach

### Your Closing Statement (模板)
> "Based on what you've shared, I'll prepare a proposal tailored to your requirements and send it over by [date]. Shall I send it to this email, or is there a preferred contact?"

---

## PHASE 6: POST-CALL ACTIONS

- [ ] Log all gathered requirements in HubSpot contact record immediately
- [ ] Note buyer personality, communication style, and decision-making dynamics
- [ ] Mark deal stage appropriately in CRM
- [ ] Set follow-up reminder before end of day
- [ ] Update any relevant trading notes or deal memos

---

## QUICK REFERENCE CARD

### Questions to Ask Before You Reveal Anything:
1. What specifications do you need?
2. What volume do you require and how often?
3. Where does it need to be delivered?
4. What payment terms work for you?
5. Who is your current supplier and when does your contract end?
6. What is your decision timeline?
7. Who else is involved in this decision?

### Your Leverage Is Gone If You:
- Email pricing in first response
- Accept "just give me a ballpark" requests
- Reveal before confirming specifications
- Show enthusiasm before understanding their urgency

---

## SOURCE PRINCIPLE

> This protocol is based on the zero-information-first sales methodology. The seller who provides pricing before qualification is selling blind — offering concessions before knowing the buyer's constraints, urgency, or leverage. In commodities, where price is a variable function of many inputs, premature disclosure is the #1 mistake that costs deals and margins.
